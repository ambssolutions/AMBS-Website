Where double entry comes from

Nobody sets out to type the same thing four times. It happens because each tool was added for a good reason: Xero for the accounts, a spreadsheet for the job list, a calendar for the crew, email for the customer. None of them knows about the others, so a person carries the information between them. Usually that person is the owner, usually at night.

The cost is not only the hours. It is the invoice that went out with the wrong price because the spreadsheet was updated and Xero was not, and the job that was booked twice because the calendar lagged behind the form.

What we connect

Xero

Draft invoices from accepted quotes or completed jobs. Supplier bills from emails, ready to approve. Payments reflected on the job sheet. Contacts kept in step with your client list. We work within Xero's own connection rules, in your own Xero account.

Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace

Outlook or Gmail, Excel or Google Sheets, Teams or Google Chat, OneDrive, SharePoint or Google Drive. An enquiry email becomes a row in the sheet. A completed job in the sheet becomes a calendar entry and a customer email. A weekly summary lands in Teams or Chat on Monday morning.

Forms, booking and job tools

Website forms, booking systems, point-of-sale and job management tools, where they can export to a spreadsheet or send an email. That covers most tools small businesses use, and we tell you honestly in the free session when it does not.

Common connections we build

  • Accepted quote → draft Xero invoice → confirmation email to the customer
  • Supplier bill email → Xero bill draft with the PDF attached
  • Website enquiry → client list → a reply draft in your inbox
  • Completed job → invoice, calendar and the weekly numbers sheet
  • Xero payment received → job marked paid, thank-you email sent
  • Bookings → reminders by text or email the day before

How it works

  1. Free session. We watch how a job moves through your tools and write down every place it gets typed again.
  2. Written plan. Which connection to build first, what it will do, and an hours estimate. Yours to keep.
  3. Build and test. Usually one to two weeks, in your own accounts, tested on real jobs before it goes live.
  4. Keep it running. Fixes and tweaks are free for the first 30 days. After that, support is a monthly plan or pay as you go.

Your data and privacy

Connections carry customer names, amounts and sometimes bank details. They are handled under the Privacy Act 2020, data we store for you is hosted in New Zealand or Australia, and it is never used to train anyone's AI model.
