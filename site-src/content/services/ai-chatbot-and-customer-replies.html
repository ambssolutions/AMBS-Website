Why customers want a quick answer

Most enquiries arrive when you are busy: on a job, with a client, or after hours. The questions are usually simple. Are you open on Saturday? Do you come to Albany? Roughly what does it cost? How do I book?

When nobody answers for a day, many people simply ask the next business on the list. A quick, accurate answer keeps them with you, and it does not have to mean you are glued to your phone.

Two ways we help

A chat helper on your website

A small "Ask us" button sits on every page. Visitors type a question and get an answer straight away, written from your own approved wording: your services, areas, opening hours, policies and how to book. Common questions can be offered as one-tap buttons.

When the question is outside what you have approved, the chat says so and offers the next step instead: book a call, leave a message or ring you. Messages arrive in your inbox like any other enquiry, with the visitor's question attached.

AI-drafted replies in your inbox

For emails and form enquiries, AI can read the message, pick out what the person is asking and draft a reply in your tone, using your price guide and policies. You read it, change anything you like, and send. Routine replies, such as booking confirmations, can go straight out once you are happy with how they work.

Answers in your customers' languages

Auckland customers speak many languages. A visitor who writes in Chinese, Hindi, Punjabi or te reo Māori can get an answer in that language, built from the answers you approved in English. Our own chat helper on this site works this way, so you can try it before deciding.

What a chat helper usually answers

  • Opening hours, location and the areas you cover
  • What you do and what you do not do
  • Rough prices or how pricing works
  • How booking works, and a link to book
  • Payment options, cancellations and refunds
  • What to bring, how long it takes, and what happens next

You can see every question visitors asked, including the ones it could not answer, so you know which answers to add next. Over a few weeks it covers most of what customers actually ask.

Where a person should stay in charge

Some messages need judgement: a complaint, a quote for an unusual job, anything to do with money owed or health details. We set these up so the draft waits for a person to check it. You decide which replies go straight out and which wait for you.

Built on the tools you already use

Email replies work inside Microsoft 365 (Outlook) or Google Workspace (Gmail). Enquiries can be recorded in a Google Sheet, an Excel file or your client list, and booking links point to the system you already use. You keep the accounts and the logins.

Your data and privacy

Questions and messages can include personal information. They are handled under the Privacy Act 2020, data we store for you is hosted in New Zealand or Australia, and it is never used to train anyone's AI model. Our guide to the Privacy Act 2020 and automation sets out what to check before you start.

How it works

  1. Free session. We look at the questions you get most and where they arrive: website, email, phone or social media.
  2. Your answers. We write the answers together, in your words, and agree what should always come to a person.
  3. Build and test. Usually one to two weeks. We test it on real questions before it goes live.
  4. Improve. Fixes and tweaks are free for the first 30 days, while we add answers for the questions people actually ask.
