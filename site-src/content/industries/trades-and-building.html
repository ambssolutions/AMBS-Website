What the repetitive work looks like in trades

A typical day starts at the merchant, runs across two or three sites and ends in the ute writing notes on the back of a docket. The quote for the bathroom you looked at on Monday still is not written. Two invoices from last week are sitting as drafts because you have not had time to add the materials. And there is a customer from a month ago who has gone quiet about paying.

Builders, electricians, plumbers, painters, roofers and landscapers will recognise a version of this. The paperwork is not difficult, but it waits until the evening or the weekend, and every delay pushes your cash further away.

The work between the job and the money follows the same steps nearly every time, which is the kind of work automation suits.

Quotes written up from site notes

Quoting is often the biggest time sink, and a slow quote can lose the job. Here is how a typical setup works:

  1. Capture on site. You leave a voice memo, snap a few photos or jot notes in a form on your phone while you are still standing in the customer's driveway.
  2. A draft is built. The notes are turned into line items using your price list, labour rates and standard terms. Anything unclear is marked for you to fill in.
  3. You check it. The draft arrives in your inbox or as a quote in your accounting software. You adjust the numbers and approve it.
  4. It goes out and is followed up. The customer gets a tidy quote, and if they have not replied in a week you get a nudge to follow up.

Your price list matters. The better your standard rates and common items are written down, the closer the first draft will be. Getting that list in order is often useful in its own right.

Job invoices into your accounts

Once a job is approved or finished, the information you need for the invoice already exists: the accepted quote, the variations and the materials. The automation pulls those together, adds the extras you recorded on the job, works out GST at 15% and creates a draft invoice in Xero for you to approve. Supplier bills for materials can be picked up from your email and entered as draft bills in Xero so fewer are missed. Our guide to automating quotes and invoices goes through this step by step.

Chasing late payments

Nobody likes ringing customers about money. A payment reminder routine takes the awkwardness out:

  1. Overdue invoices are spotted daily. The automation checks your accounting software each morning.
  2. Reminders follow your rules. A friendly note a few days after the due date, a firmer one later, all in wording you approve.
  3. It stops when they pay. Once the payment is matched in your accounts, the reminders end.
  4. You hear about the stubborn ones. Invoices that stay unpaid after the last reminder land on a short list for you to call.

Other trade tasks worth automating

  • Booking confirmations and an on-my-way text to the customer on the morning of the job.
  • Timesheets from the crew collected on their phones and totalled for payroll.
  • Job photos filed into the right customer folder automatically, ready for warranty questions later.
  • Variation requests recorded on site and added to the final invoice.
  • Reminders for vehicle servicing, tool tagging and licence renewals.
  • New enquiries from your website sorted and answered with a request for photos and a suitable time to visit.
