What the repetitive work looks like in a small law firm

Small law firms, sole practitioners and conveyancing practices all share a similar week. Part of it is the work clients value: understanding their situation, advising them and preparing the documents that matter. The rest is administration that has to be done carefully but does not need a lawyer's expertise.

Most of that administration falls into three buckets. Getting new clients set up, which means sending engagement documents and chasing signed forms, identity documents and the information you need before you can start. Writing up file notes after each call or meeting, usually at the end of a long day. And keeping clients updated and key dates in view, such as the steps leading up to a settlement.

In a busy month this admin grows faster than the legal work. That is where automation helps most, with the lawyer still in charge of what goes on the file.

New client onboarding and document chasing

  1. A checklist per matter. When a new matter opens, such as a property purchase or a will, the client gets your engagement documents, a list of exactly what you need from them and a secure way to upload it.
  2. Uploads are recognised. As signed forms, identity documents and other files arrive, they are named, filed in the right matter folder and ticked off the list.
  3. Reminders only ask for what is missing. On a schedule you set, the client gets a friendly note listing the items still outstanding, in wording you approve.
  4. You see the status at a glance. A simple view shows which matters are ready to start, which are waiting on the client and which need a phone call.

Which documents you need, and what checks you must do on them, is for your firm to decide. We build the checklist to match your own process.

File notes drafted from calls

With the client's knowledge, a call or meeting is recorded or transcribed. AI then drafts a file note in your usual structure: who was present, what was discussed, the client's instructions, any advice given and agreed next steps. The draft goes to the lawyer to review, correct and approve, and only then is it saved to the client file. Action items can become tasks with due dates. The lawyer stays responsible for the content; the automation saves the typing and the blank page.

Settle the privacy and confidentiality questions first. Client information in a law firm is often sensitive. Before recording any calls, decide how clients are told, where recordings and transcripts are stored, who can see them and how long they are kept, and check these against your own professional obligations. Key date reminders and client updates

Clients often ring to ask where their matter is up to, and the answer is usually routine. A typical setup keeps a simple register of each matter's key dates, entered by your team. As a date gets close, the right person gets a reminder, and anything overdue is flagged. When a matter reaches a set stage, such as documents signed or a settlement date confirmed, a short update to the client is drafted in your wording for your team to check and send. The automation does not work out deadlines or decide what a date should be; it makes sure the dates your team has entered are not missed.

Other tasks worth automating

  • New enquiries from your website sorted by type of matter, with a reply asking for the details you need and a time for a first meeting.
  • Client emails filed against the right matter, with routine questions drafted for reply.
  • Your own invoices drafted in Xero from completed work, with polite reminders for overdue fees.
  • Weekly work-in-progress summaries showing matters waiting on clients versus matters waiting on you.
  • Reminders for annual reviews, such as checking whether a client's will or other documents need updating.
  • Closed matter files checked for missing documents before they are archived.

Tools we connect for law firms

  • Microsoft 365: Outlook for client email and calendar, Excel for checklists and registers, Teams for internal messages, and OneDrive or SharePoint for matter folders.
  • Google Workspace: Gmail and Google Calendar, Google Sheets for checklists and registers, and Google Drive for matter folders.
  • Xero for your own billing and payments.
