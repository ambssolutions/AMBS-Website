What the repetitive work looks like in a clinic or salon

In a physio practice, a hair salon, a beauty or nail studio, a massage clinic or a small allied health practice, the day is booked in back-to-back slots. Admin gets squeezed into the gaps: confirming tomorrow's appointments, chasing a new client who has not filled in their intake form, and trying to call back the number that rang three times while you were with someone.

Every missed step has a cost. An unconfirmed appointment can turn into a no-show. A form that was not completed means questions eating into the start of a session. A missed call can be a new client who books somewhere else.

These tasks repeat with almost every appointment, which makes them well suited to automation. The system handles the routine messages, and your team handles the people.

Booking reminders and rebooking

  1. Confirmation on booking. As soon as an appointment is made, the client gets a confirmation with the time, address, parking notes and anything they need to bring.
  2. Reminder before the visit. A reminder goes out a day or two before, with a simple way to confirm, reschedule or cancel.
  3. Gaps are filled. When someone cancels, clients on your waitlist can be offered the slot.
  4. Rebooking after the visit. Clients who usually come back every few weeks get a friendly prompt when they are due, which helps keep regulars from drifting away.

Our guide to reducing no-shows with booking reminders covers timing and wording in more detail.

New client forms and files

Paper intake forms get lost, and typing them into your system takes time. A typical setup sends new clients a short online form when they book. It asks the questions you need, such as contact details, relevant history, allergies or consent, and checks that required fields are filled. The completed form is saved to the client's file, a copy is kept as a PDF, and you are told if anything needs your attention before the appointment, such as a medical condition or a patch test that is due.

Health information needs extra care. Decide who can see each form, where it is stored and how long you keep it before you automate anything. Our Privacy Act 2020 guide lists what to check, and your professional body may have its own requirements.

Missed call follow-ups

  1. A missed call is detected. Where your phone system allows it, an unanswered call during opening hours is logged with the caller's number.
  2. A text goes back quickly. The caller gets a short message saying you are with a client, with a link to book online or a request to reply with what they need.
  3. Replies are sorted. Simple questions such as prices, hours or availability can be answered automatically, using the approach in our guide to AI customer replies.
  4. The rest come to you. Anything that needs a person lands on a short call-back list for your next gap.

Other tasks worth automating

  • Aftercare instructions sent automatically after particular treatments.
  • Review requests sent to clients after their visit.
  • Gift voucher sales recorded and tracked, with reminders before they expire.
  • Daily takings and appointment numbers summarised for the owner each evening.
  • Product stock for retail shelves counted and reordered on a schedule.
  • Staff commission or contractor hours totalled at the end of each pay period.

Tools we usually connect for clinics and salons

  • Microsoft 365: Outlook for confirmations and client emails, Excel for simple reports, and OneDrive or SharePoint for client files.
  • Google Workspace: Gmail and Google Calendar, Google Sheets for simple reports, and Google Drive for client files.
  • Xero for invoices and takings.

We do not connect to your booking system directly. Where it sends you booking emails or can export to Excel or Google Sheets, we work from what it already produces.
