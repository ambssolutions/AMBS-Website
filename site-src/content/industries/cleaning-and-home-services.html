What the repetitive work looks like in cleaning and home services

Home cleaners, office and commercial cleaners, gardeners and lawn mowing rounds, pest control operators and window washers share a similar week. Most of the work is regular: the same houses every fortnight, the same office every night, the same shopfront windows every month. Between the jobs are new enquiries, often a photo and a question sent late in the evening.

The admin around that work never quite finishes. Customers need a reminder before the visit. Someone asks to skip a week or move to Thursday. A new enquiry needs a price. Invoices for last month's visits have to go out, and a few customers always need a nudge to pay.

Because so much of this repeats on a schedule, it is a good fit for automation. The routine messages and paperwork run on their own, and you deal with the customers and the decisions.

Regular visit reminders and changes

  1. Each customer's pattern is set once. Weekly, fortnightly, monthly or a seasonal treatment, kept in a schedule in Excel or Google Sheets, or worked from what your scheduling tool already exports.
  2. A reminder goes out before the visit. The customer gets a short message the day before, with the time window and anything they need to do, such as leaving the side gate open or keeping pets inside.
  3. Changes come to one place. Requests to skip, move or cancel come in by reply or a short form, are checked against the schedule and sent to you to approve.
  4. The team gets the updated list. Each person sees their jobs for the day on their phone, with addresses and job notes, and only the affected person is told about a change.

Access details need care. Key locations, alarm codes and gate codes should only reach the person doing that job. Decide who can see what before you automate anything, and our guide to the Privacy Act 2020 and automation lists what to check.

Quotes from enquiry photos

A slow reply to an enquiry can lose the job. An enquiry form on your website asks for the details you need to price the work, such as the size of the house or section, how often they want the service, and a few photos. The automation turns those details into a draft quote using your own price list and standard wording, and marks anything unclear for you to fill in. You check and adjust the price, then approve it. If the customer has not replied after a few days, you get a nudge to follow up. Our guide to automating quotes and invoices goes through this step by step.

Write your prices down. The clearer your standard rates are, such as per room, per hour or per visit, the closer the first draft will be. Getting that list in order is often useful in its own right.

Invoices and late payments for repeat work

  1. Completed visits are recorded. When a job is marked done on the phone, it is added to that customer's list for billing, along with any extras such as an oven clean or green waste removal.
  2. Draft invoices are created. Per visit or once a month, whichever you prefer, with GST at 15% worked out and the draft created in Xero for you to approve.
  3. Reminders follow your rules. A friendly note a few days after the due date and a firmer one later, all in wording you approve. Once the payment is matched in your accounts, the reminders stop.
  4. You hear about the stubborn ones. Invoices still unpaid after the last reminder land on a short list for you to call.

Other tasks worth automating

  • Job checklists and before-and-after photos completed on a phone and filed in the right customer folder.
  • Treatment records for pest control jobs, with a reminder when the next treatment is due.
  • Timesheets from the team collected on their phones and totalled for payroll.
  • Review requests sent to customers after a visit.
  • Reminders for vehicle servicing, equipment checks and certificate renewal dates.

Tools we connect for home services

  • Xero for quotes, invoices, supplier bills and payments.
  • Microsoft 365: Outlook for customer emails and enquiries, Excel for schedules and price lists, and OneDrive or SharePoint for job photos and checklists.
  • Google Workspace: Gmail and Google Calendar, Google Sheets for schedules and price lists, and Google Drive for job photos and checklists.

We do not connect to job scheduling or booking software directly. Where it sends you emails or can export to Excel or Google Sheets, we work from what it already produces. Our guide to connecting your business apps explains how information moves between these without retyping.

Digital tools that help

  • A website that wins trust. The services you offer, the areas you cover, photos of real work and an enquiry form that asks for what you need to quote, fast on a phone.
  • A staff portal where the team sees the day's jobs, access notes for their own jobs, checklists, and uploads photos from their phones.
  • A customer portal where regular customers see upcoming visits, ask for changes and view their invoices.
  • A live dashboard showing jobs done, quotes out and money owed for the week.
