Automation for cafés and takeaways

Running a café or takeaway means the admin happens after close, on your phone, between orders. We take the rosters, supplier orders and review replies off your plate so less of it spills into your evenings.

What the repetitive work looks like in a café

The coffee machine goes on before six, the lunch rush runs until two, and the admin waits until everyone has gone home. Then it is the roster for next week, a text from someone who cannot do Saturday, the milk and bread orders, a pile of supplier invoices in the inbox, and a two-star Google review you have been meaning to answer since Tuesday.

None of it is hard. It is just constant, and it nearly always lands on the owner or the manager. In a takeaway the pattern is the same, with more evening shifts and more delivery platform messages on top.

Most of this work follows the same steps every week, which makes it a strong fit for automation: your systems do the routine part, and you only step in for the decisions.

Rosters and shift swap messages

Most of the time on rosters is not writing them. It is the messages afterwards: who can swap, who is already on, whether a junior can close alone. A typical setup works like this:

  1. Staff ask in one place. Instead of texting you, they send a swap or leave request through a short form or by email.
  2. The request is checked. The automation looks at the roster sheet to see who is free, who is already near their hours and whether the shift still has a barista and someone who can close.
  3. You approve it. You get a short summary on your phone. Approve it and the roster updates.
  4. Everyone is told. Both staff members get a confirmation, and the updated roster goes out to the team.

Supplier orders and invoice entry

Ordering is usually a mental calculation done at closing time. We turn that into a simple routine:

  1. Quick count on a phone. At close, someone enters counts for the items you reorder often, such as milk, cups, bread and produce.
  2. Orders drafted per supplier. The count is compared with the levels you want on hand, and an order for each supplier is drafted.
  3. You check and send. Orders go out by email once you approve them, or arrive as a list you can read out to suppliers who prefer the phone.
  4. Invoices handled when they arrive. Supplier invoices emailed to you are picked up, checked against what you ordered and entered as draft bills in Xero for you to approve.

Start with your biggest three suppliers. They often account for most of the invoices. Smaller suppliers can be added once the routine is running smoothly.

Google reviews and customer questions

Reviews and questions matter for a café, but answering them is easy to put off. On Google, that mostly means replying to reviews. The automation picks up new reviews and drafts a reply in the tone you use. You see the draft on your phone and approve, edit or skip it. Common questions that come in through your website or by email, such as opening hours, gluten-free options or whether you take bookings, can be answered from information you have approved. Complaints and anything unusual are flagged for you to handle personally. Social media messages are not covered, so those stay with you. Other café tasks worth automating

Once the first automation is running, these are common next steps:

  • Timesheets totalled from the roster and sent to whoever does payroll each week.
  • Catering and large orders captured from a website form into a single order list with deposit reminders.
  • Daily takings summarised and sent to your phone each night, where your POS can email its end-of-day report or export to Excel or Google Sheets.
  • Cleaning and temperature checklists completed on a phone and filed automatically, so your records are easy to find when someone asks.
  • Reminders for staff training refreshers and certificate renewal dates.
  • A weekly note of best and worst sellers, so you can adjust the cabinet and the menu.

Tools we usually connect for cafés

You do not need new software. In cafés and takeaways we connect:

  • Microsoft 365: Outlook for supplier orders and invoices, Excel for rosters and stock levels, and Teams or OneDrive for the team.
  • Google Workspace: Gmail for supplier orders and invoices, Google Sheets for rosters and stock levels, and Google Calendar and Drive.
  • Xero for supplier bills and payroll figures.

For your POS, we work from the sales reports it can already email or export to Excel or Google Sheets, rather than connecting to it directly. If you are curious how these connections work behind the scenes, our guide to connecting your business apps covers it step by step.

Digital tools that help cafés

Alongside automation, a few digital tools make a real difference in hospitality:

  • A fast, mobile website with your current menu, hours and a catering enquiry form that actually reaches you. Plenty of people check on their phone before they walk in.
  • A simple staff portal where the team can see the roster, request swaps, read the opening and closing checklists and find the day's specials.
  • A live dashboard showing sales, wage costs and supplier spend side by side, so you can see how a week is tracking before it ends.

Common questions

Will an automation post replies to my reviews without me seeing them?

Not unless you want it to. We usually set it up so each reply is drafted in your voice and sent to your phone to approve, edit or skip. Negative or unusual reviews are flagged for you to answer personally.

Do I need a rostering app for this to work?

No. Many cafés run their roster in a spreadsheet, and we can work with that. If you already use a rostering or POS system, we do not replace it. Where it can export to Excel or Google Sheets, or sends you emails, we work from what it already produces.

Can it handle suppliers who still take orders by phone or text?

Partly. The order can still be worked out automatically from your stock count, and then sent to you as a ready-to-read list for that supplier. Anything that can go by email goes by email, and the rest you read out or send yourself.
